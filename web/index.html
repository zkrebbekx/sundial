sundial

an SLA clock — a clickable playground

Repo Design API docs

sundial measures elapsed working time over a business schedule. A sundial only advances in daylight; an SLA clock only advances during business hours — nights, weekends and holidays do not count against a “resolve within 8 working hours” target. It is a pure clock and nothing else: you supply now, and it tells you how much working time has elapsed, when the budget runs out, which escalation levels have fired, and how a pause moves the due date.

Everything below runs in your tab over the pure library compiled to WebAssembly — no backend, no network. Instants cross in as civil-time strings in the schedule’s zone (2025-03-07T16:00:00), so a 09:00–17:00 window is eight working hours whether or not the clocks changed that day. Reload to reset. Built from zkrebbekx/sundial.

SLA target

The budget is the working time the SLA allows from the start. The due marker is Add(start, budget) — the wall-clock instant that much working time runs out, weekends and holidays skipped.

Escalation levels — write-once

Ascending thresholds fire once each, at the working-time mark they name. The fired-at instant is a pure function of the schedule, so dragging now back and forth never re-fires a level or moves its instant.

Pause / resume

A ticket waiting on the customer freezes the clock. The excluded time is pushed onto the end, so the due date moves later by exactly the working time inside the pause.

  • A completed pause shifts due later by exactly its working time.
  • A pause over a night or a weekend holds no working time, so it shifts nothing.
  • An open pause (no resume) freezes elapsed at the pause instant.

DST-safe — civil time, not wall-clock

Windows are defined in civil time, so 09:00–17:00 is eight working hours whether or not the clocks changed. A budget crossing US spring-forward lands on the right civil instant while the real span is one hour shorter.
